Piqeras in five languages

The trip app now speaks Albanian, English, Greek, Romanian and Italian — and shows each member the clock they are actually on. Plus a share button that sends the countdown and the whole travel plan to the group chat as one picture.

Open the app

Five languages, all the way through

Every string is translated — around 230 of them per language — including the copy in push notifications. Each bundle is typed against the Albanian one, so a key added later and left untranslated is a build error rather than an Albanian sentence sitting in a Greek screen. Place names stay put: Piqeras and Sarandë are never translated.

Dates follow the language with the grammar each one actually uses. Greek months take the genitive after a day number — 24 Σεπτεμβρίου, not Σεπτέμβριος — while Romanian and Italian keep months in lower case mid-sentence.

Each member sees their own clock

The trip leaves at one absolute moment, 2026-09-23T15:00Z. What that moment is called depends on where you are reading, so the app now names it correctly for each language instead of showing everyone Albanian time.

English stays on the trip's own clock — it is a language, not a country. Italy shares Albania's offset, so Italian changes nothing today; it is in the table so the mapping is a complete answer rather than a list of exceptions. Where a member's clock genuinely disagrees, the screen says so underneath the date, so nobody quietly reads an hour wrong.

The departure picker reads and writes in that same clock. If it had only converted the display, every Greek member's vote would have landed an hour off — silently, and only for them.

Deliberate, and worth knowing

The Greek and Romanian countdown runs one hour short, at your request. A countdown is the gap between two moments, and changing timezone moves both ends together, so the true remaining time is identical everywhere. The consequence to expect: those two languages reach zero — and flip to "we left" — an hour before the group actually leaves.

It is written as "the same clock reading, in their zone" rather than a hardcoded minus-one- hour, so it cannot drift if any of the three countries changes its daylight-saving rules. Both the code and docs/TIMEZONE.md record that this was a decision, so nobody later reads it as a bug and reverses it.

The countdown is yours

The group does not leave together — this trip already has three departures across three days — so the countdown is the time until you go. Pick a departure and the numbers start counting to it; until you have picked one they count to the trip's own departure, as before.

When your time differs from the trip's, a line under the countdown says which one it is counting to, so the big number never quietly disagrees with the date printed above it. Somebody who answered "not coming" has no departure of their own and keeps seeing the trip's — which is also what they need if they change their mind. Nobody else's vote can move your countdown.

The shared card deliberately stays on the trip's departure: it is a picture of the group's plan, and a countdown that only makes sense on one person's phone would be wrong in everybody else's chat.

One tap sends the whole plan

A share icon sits on the countdown screen and in the top bar of every other screen. Tapping it opens the phone's share sheet with a picture attached — straight into WhatsApp or Viber, with no prefilled caption to delete first. Where a browser cannot share files it saves the picture instead and says so. Files are dated, piqeras-2026-09-12.png, so a full gallery still sorts.

On the countdown screen the picture is composed for sharing rather than photographed. A screenshot would carry a row of counts and three buttons nobody can tap in an image; what the group actually wants is the countdown and the travel plan. So the card keeps the header and the countdown, and in place of the tally it lists everybody — under the departure they chose, then the ones not coming, then the ones who have not answered.

Everything on the card follows the reader's language and clock — a Greek member's copy says 18:00 · 23 Σεπτεμβρίου for the same departure. The grouping itself stays keyed to Albanian time, so two people on one departure never split into separate rows just because somebody reads the card in Greek. Names come from the approved roster only: people who have signed in but are not approved yet are never named on a picture the group forwards around.

Every other screen still shares a photograph of itself, at whatever you have scrolled to, with the share button left out of the shot. The drawing library loads only when somebody first taps share, so the app itself grew by 7 KB.

Also this session

  • Notifications were about to go out in the wrong language

    The code that records your language on your account collapsed everything except English to Albanian, so a Greek member would have been stored as Albanian and pushed notifications in a language they had switched away from. Fixed, with a test that fails if it ever regresses.

  • Greek capitals carried accents they should not

    The welcome line rendered ΚΑΛΏΣ ΉΡΘΕΣ. Greek drops accents in all-caps, and browsers only apply that rule when the page declares its language — which the app only did after you changed language by hand. Now correct on first load.

  • A second administrator

    Organizer promotion works from Paneli i organizatorit → Organizatorët. The list was reading from all signed-in accounts, including people not yet approved — promoting one would have failed with a vague error. It now lists only approved members.

  • Storage now allows the app to read its own photo

    Taking a picture of a screen containing the trip photo requires the storage bucket to permit it. It now allows image reads from the app's own three addresses and nothing else, recorded in storage.cors.json.

How it was checked

224 app tests
184 security-rule tests
122 backend tests
530 total, all passing

Beyond the tests: the sharing was exercised in a real browser — signed in, in Greek and in Albanian, picture taken, picture examined, repeated scrolled to the bottom of a long list. That is how two defects surfaced that no test would have caught: a capture that photographed the top of the page while you were reading the bottom, and a rasteriser that drew 18:00 as 18 :00. The files being served by piqeras.web.app were byte-compared against the build that passed the tests.
